A kikoi is a rectangle. That is very nearly the whole design brief, and it is
the reason the thing has outlasted almost every object sold alongside it.

## What it does

It dries you. It covers a window. It carries a child. It becomes a tablecloth
when someone arrives unannounced, and a gift when you leave. None of these are
features. They are consequences of the object having been left alone.

> The best-designed thing in the room is usually the one nobody designed
> recently.

## Against specialisation

Most contemporary objects are made narrower on purpose, because a narrow object
can be sold twice. A towel that is only a towel needs a companion. A rectangle
of cotton needs nothing.

There is a version of intentional living that means buying the correct
specialised tool for every task. There is another version that means owning
fewer things and asking more of them. This is a case for the second.
