The route starts at ten past five. Not five, and not quarter past. Ten past,
because that is when the first of the regulars is standing outside the bakery
on the corner, and being there before her would be a waste of four minutes.

## The route is not a map

He does not think of the city as streets. He thinks of it as a sequence of
people, each of whom is somewhere predictable at a predictable time, and the
gaps between them are the parts he can sell to anyone.

> Nine years is long enough that the road tells you things. You stop looking at
> it and start listening to it.

## What nine years buys

Repetition is usually described as the enemy of attention. Watching someone
work a route they have worked two thousand times suggests the opposite. He
notices a new pothole within a day. He knows which junction has changed its
timing. The knowledge is not written down anywhere and would take another nine
years to replace.
